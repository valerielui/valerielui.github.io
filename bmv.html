Background

Black Mamas’s Village needed a smoother system to manage volunteers with their no-code CRM and reduce admin load.

Black Mamas’s Village (BMV) is a nonprofit in Austin, TX, using Airtable and Stacker as their CRM. But its unintuitive UI in the CRM forced staff to manually support volunteers at every turn.

As the sole designer, I partnered with the co-founder to tackle these gaps and turn the system into a self-serve portal that worked for everyone.

BMV’s CRM that uses a Stacker front-end UI and Airtable backend database.

Discovery & Strategy

Over 8 weeks, I led a collaborative discovery process with the co-founder.

In our sessions, we:
1

Mapped out user and admin pain points in Airtable and Stacker

2

Audited workflows to identify duplication and inefficiencies

3

Aligned on a streamlined, user-first portal vision

A future state screen flow I created for the coordinator persona

The Approach

I had to be creative — creating an intuitive user experience with Stacker’s limited capabilities was not the easiest.

To tackle this, I:
1

Designed new user flows and wireframes in Figma to visualize and test potential improvements.

2

Created sandbox pages directly in Stacker to validate these ideas with the co-founder before launching live changes.

3

Switched to a third-party form (which integrates with Stacker) for event creation, to allow conditional display of questions that are relevant, improving both admin and volunteer UX.

The Solution

Working within Stacker’s no-code limitations, I created a streamlined, self-serve volunteer portal with two key focuses.

My key focuses were:
1

Allowing volunteers and coordinators to self manage events & registration

2

Eliminating volunteer guesswork by reframing critical information

The Takeaway

Designing within no-code constraints taught me how to find creative workarounds to deliver real user impact.

This project was my first time using Stacker and Airtable, and it taught me the power of designing within constraints.

Working within a no-code platform meant I had to get creative with solutions. This included integrating FillOut to bypass Stacker’s form limitations, configuring new fields on the Airtable database to create user-friendly displays of information.

These creative workarounds let me build a user-first experience that directly supported BMV’s mission and freed up staff to do more good.
